# Rosalyn — Story Arc Outline

### 3 Arcs · 9 Interactions · Confusion → Realization → Acceptance

---

## Character Foundation

**Rosalyn** is an incredibly timid and shy customer who only visits the store when it is quiet. Unlike the other characters, she has no connection to the neighborhood, doesn't know Kap or Ana, and doesn't seem to know much about herself either. Her memories are heavily fragmented. While she appears as a normal, albeit very pale, young woman, clues throughout her dialogue hint that she is actually a wandering spirit (a "White Lady" of Philippine folklore) who is trapped in a loop of forgotten trauma. The player's store acts as a safe, quiet anchor point where she can slowly piece together her fragmented identity—and eventually find the courage to cross over.

### Dialogue Filter (Global)

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Core Want** | To find an anchor — something physical, something warm, something that proves she still exists and is worth the space she takes up. |
| **Strategy** | Excessive politeness as armor. She apologizes constantly, sometimes for things that don't require apology, and she does this so automatically that she'll occasionally catch herself and apologize for apologizing. But beneath the self-effacement is someone quietly, earnestly *grateful* for every small thing that works — a bottle she can hold, a counter she can rest her hands on, a person who doesn't look through her. |
| **What She Hides** | She *knows* she is dead. She has known for a while. What she doesn't know — and is deeply embarrassed about — is *why* she hasn't crossed over. She has been a ghost long enough that she feels she should have figured out the exit by now, and the fact that she hasn't makes her feel like she is doing death wrong. Her amnesia is specific: the accident itself, and whatever she was waiting for on that highway. |
| **Voice** | Soft, quiet, and hesitant. Uses lots of ellipses ("..."), trailing thoughts, and filler words ("Um," "Sorry," "I know this is strange"). Rarely uses Filipino words, reflecting her disconnect from her own identity — except when a specific item triggers a sudden, sharp memory. Occasionally makes a very small, dry observation about her condition — she's been a ghost long enough to have developed tiny resigned non-jokes about it — and then immediately apologizes for making them. |

### The Arc at a Glance

```
ARC 1 — THE FRAGMENTED VISITOR  →  ARC 2 — PIECING IT TOGETHER    →  ARC 3 — ACCEPTANCE & CROSSING
She is terrified and confused.     She realizes the truth.           She finds peace.
Player acts as a patient anchor.   Player provides nostalgic items.  Player helps her cross over.
```

**Collectible Reward:** *Faded Sampaguita* — A dried, perfectly preserved Sampaguita flower. Despite being brittle, it still smells faintly of fresh rain.

## ARC 1 — "THE FRAGMENTED VISITOR"

**Thematic Purpose:** Establish her extreme anxiety, her flight-risk nature, and her amnesia. The store's mundane transactions serve as a grounding mechanism for her frantic mind.

**Arc-End State:** The player learns that her memory is broken, but they build enough trust that she stops running away.

---

### Chapter 1 — *"The Flight"*

**Scene Beat:** First meeting. The player is behind the counter. Rosalyn approaches, but her nerve fails at the last moment. She almost makes a purchase — she gets impressively close — and then retreats entirely.

**Information to Communicate:**

- She is overwhelmingly shy.
- She wants to buy something, but loses her nerve.
- She is a "flight risk" but a very polite one.

**Character Filter — What She Wants:**
To buy something, *anything*, to prove to herself that she still can. She's been working up to this for a while.

**What Else Is Happening:**
She's been standing outside the store for a noticeable amount of time before coming in. She enters holding her hands together very carefully, as if she's concentrating on keeping them solid.

**Key Beat:**
> *(She enters, and takes careful, deliberate steps up to the counter. She almost makes it. She actually makes it. She opens her mouth.)*
> *"Hello. I would like to purchase a— um."*
> *(She looks at the shelves. She looks at the player. She looks back at the shelves.)*
> *"I'm sorry. I had it. I had exactly what I was going to say. I'm taking up your time."*
> *(She gestures vaguely at the store as if to say 'carry on,' and then retreats — quickly, but with a very small, apologetic wave goodbye.)*

**Information Out:** Establishes her shyness, but also her *effort* — the wave goodbye is charming. The player should feel inclined to look for her again.

---

### Chapter 2 — *"The Attempt"*

**Scene Beat:** She returns, already apologizing before she reaches the counter. She tries to complete a purchase, and she actually gets further this time — far enough that the specific, peculiar reason she stops is now visible. The comedy is in how earnestly she explains her perfectly reasonable ghost concern.

**Information to Communicate:**

- She knows she's dead, and more specifically, she knows the *practical inconveniences* of being dead.
- She is trying to be brave but has a very legitimate problem.
- She treats her ghost logistics with an embarrassed pragmatism.

**Character Filter — What She Wants:**
To finally buy something. She's been thinking about why it went wrong last time and she came prepared with a plan, except the plan has a flaw.

**What Else Is Happening:**
She arrives with more visible determination. She's thought about this.

**Key Beat:**
> *"Good day. I'm sorry about before. I've been... I've been practicing."*
> *(She places her hands on the counter with great concentration, then looks at them, surprised they stayed).*
> *"Oh. Good. Okay. I would like to buy— um. I have coins. They're in my pocket. Except."*
> *(She looks up, genuinely embarrassed.)*
> *"Sometimes I can hold things and sometimes I can't. And I can't always tell which kind of day it is until I try. And I didn't want to drop the coins on the floor and then... go through the floor. In front of you. That seemed impolite."*
> *(She considers this for a moment.)*
> *"I'm sorry. I'll try again tomorrow. I'm sorry."*

**Information Out:** Her limitation is now specific and funny — she's worried about being impolite, not just about the coins. The player should feel fond of her, not just sorry for her.

---

### Chapter 3 — *"The Anchor"*

**Scene Beat:** She arrives with a bottle already in mind. She did it — she has a system. She is, very quietly, a little bit proud of it. The purchase succeeds, and she explains her reasoning with the earnest satisfaction of someone who spent a lot of time on a very small solution.

**Information to Communicate:**

- She has figured out a workaround.
- Cold things help her maintain physical cohesion.
- She is, in her own way, resourceful.

**Character Filter — What She Wants:**
To demonstrate that she prepared, and to prove — mostly to herself — that she can complete a normal transaction.

**Subtext Through Commerce:**
She buys [HighestDrink], cold. The act of holding the bottle successfully is a small quiet triumph.

**Key Beat:**
> *"Good day. I would like [HighestDrink], please. Cold, if you have it."*
> *(She places her coins on the counter — a little too carefully, watching them with intense focus, and then exhales when they stay put.)*
> *"I figured something out. Cold things are... easier. To hold onto. I think — and I realize this sounds strange — I think the cold gives me something to concentrate on, and the concentrating is what keeps me... present."*
> *(She accepts the bottle, holds it with both hands, and there is a brief pause while she just appreciates that it worked.)*
> *"I know that's unusual. I'm sorry. But it works. So. Thank you."*
> *(A pause.)*
> *"I'm quite pleased about this, actually."*

**Information Out:** The player now has a clear sense of her personality — she's not just sad and shy. She's a problem-solver who apologizes for solving problems. The warmth comes from her quiet pride.

## ARC 2 — "PIECING IT TOGETHER"

**Thematic Purpose:** Since she already knows she's ethereal, the tragedy is her deep isolation. The items she attempts to buy trigger her fragmented memories of *why* she is trapped.

**Arc-End State:** She pieces together the specific memory of her past that prevents her from crossing over.

---

### Chapter 4 — *"The Burn"*

**Scene Beat:** She attempts to buy hot coffee. The coffee is the setup. The specific way she explains her relationship to heat is where the character lives — it's funny and sad in the same breath.

**Information to Communicate:**

- She can't feel temperature anymore, but she keeps *trying*.
- She is self-aware about how this looks.
- She misses warmth as an idea rather than a sensation.

**Character Filter — What She Wants:**
To experience something warm — not out of denial, but out of stubborn, optimistic repetition. She keeps trying because stopping feels like giving up on something.

**Key Beat:**
> *"Could I have a hot coffee, please? Actually—"*
> *(She stops herself. She looks at the player with the expression of someone who has had this exact thought before.)*
> *"I know what you're going to observe. I know I can't feel it. I am aware."*
> *(She pauses.)*
> *"I spilled some on my hand last Tuesday. Just to check. I do that sometimes. I check. And this time — again — nothing."*
> *(She looks down at her hands.)*
> *"It's fine. I'll take a cold [HighestDrink] instead. I know that works."*
> *(A small beat. She collects the bottle. Almost to herself:)*
> *"Maybe next Tuesday."*

**Information Out:** The "maybe next Tuesday" lands the tragedy lightly. She is not wallowing — she is patiently trying. That's warmer, funnier, and more heartbreaking than pure sadness.

---

### Chapter 5 — *"The Silence"*

**Scene Beat:** She visits late at night. She has been having an observation-heavy few days and she needs to share them with someone, which means she does most of the talking — quietly, a little haltingly — and then apologizes for all of it. The observations themselves are both lonely and oddly specific.

**Information to Communicate:**

- She observes the living world from outside it.
- She has made a few small, precise notes about her condition that she cannot share with anyone else.
- The player is genuinely her only listener.

**Character Filter — What She Wants:**
To say things out loud to a person who can hear her. Not necessarily to be understood — just to be *heard*.

**Key Beat:**
> *(She approaches the counter slowly, as if she's considered whether this was a good idea all the way from the street.)*
> *"Good day. I don't have any coins tonight. I just wanted to say—"*
> *(She pauses, collecting herself.)*
> *"Out there, it's very quiet. Cars pass through me and I can't hear them. Two cats walked through me today, which is fine, I've adjusted to that. One of them stopped and looked at me for a moment, though. Just looked. I thought that was... nice."*
> *(She gives a small, careful smile — the kind that acknowledges how strange this sounds.)*
> *"I'm sorry. I know you have a store to run. I just needed to say something to someone who could hear it. Thank you for being open."*

**Information Out:** The cat detail is specific and charming — an unlikely witness in a world that doesn't see her. The player should feel both fond of her and gently sad.

---

### Chapter 6 — *"The Puddle"*

**Scene Beat:** The tonal shift. Quietly, without announcement, the warmth and gentle humor of the previous interactions make this one land harder. She's not apologizing for existing tonight. She's just telling the player what she saw.

**Information to Communicate:**

- She remembers the accident.
- The amnesia breaks specifically through her reflection in water.
- The reveal is delivered simply, without decoration.

**Character Filter — What She Wants:**
To tell someone. Not to process it out loud, just to say it to a witness. She has held this alone for as long as she has been dead.

**What Else Is Happening:**
She looks slightly damp, as if she stepped out of rain that isn't falling. She's not fidgeting. She's unusually still.

**Key Beat:**
> *"I didn't bring coins tonight. I'm sorry."*
> *(She looks at her hands. She doesn't apologize for looking at them.)*
> *"I looked in a puddle on the way here. I do that sometimes — I keep hoping my reflection will come back. It doesn't. But tonight when I looked... I remembered the rain. A very specific rain. From a very specific night."*
> *(She traces a finger over the wooden counter, barely above the surface.)*
> *"I remember a loud stop. On the highway. And then — quiet."*
> *(She looks up at the player. For the first time, she doesn't apologize.)*
> *"I finally remember why I'm still here."*

**Information Out:** The absence of an apology is the tell. The audience registers it because all the previous interactions trained them to expect one. Her stillness is the most expressive thing she's ever done.

---

## ARC 3 — "ACCEPTANCE & CROSSING"

**Thematic Purpose:** Transitioning from fear to peace. With the truth revealed, she isn't frantic anymore. The player helps her let go of the mortal world.

**Arc-End State:** She says goodbye, leaving a token of her memory behind.

---

### Chapter 7 — *"The Candy"*

**Scene Beat:** She returns noticeably calmer. The anxiety is still there — it's part of her — but it sits differently now. She knows something she didn't know before, and she carries it with a new, quiet steadiness. She buys something specific, without explaining why. Let the player sit with what that might mean.

**Information to Communicate:**

- She has processed the memory.
- She knows what she's been waiting for, but she isn't ready to say it yet.
- Something in her has settled.

**Character Filter — What She Wants:**
To buy one specific thing, and to feel it, and to be here for a moment without apologizing.

**What Else Is Happening:**
She speaks a little more slowly. The fidgeting is gone.

**Key Beat:**
> *"Good day. Could I have one of the menthol candies, please?"*
> *(She pays carefully. She takes the candy. She holds it, not eating it, just — holds it.)*
> *(A moment of quiet.)*
> *"Thank you."*
> *(She turns to go, then pauses, as if deciding something.)*
> *"I was never afraid of this store. I want you to know that. It's very warm in here. The light is very... present. I always felt it from the road."*
> *(She leaves. She doesn't apologize.)*

**Information Out:** The menthol candy is a planted detail — its meaning will land in Interaction 8. Her departure without an apology is itself information.

---

### Chapter 8 - *"Letting Go"*

**Scene Beat:** She explains the candy. She explains who she was waiting for. She does this calmly, without the old frantic apologies, because she has decided to put it down â€” the waiting, the hoping, the holding on. The smile she gives at the end of this scene is the most important moment in her arc.

**Information to Communicate:**

- Her fiance always smelled like menthol candy.
- She understands now that he has moved on.
- She is *choosing* â€” not being forced â€” to let go.

**Character Filter - What She Wants:**
To say this out loud to someone, so it's real. And then not to carry it anymore.

**Key Beat:**
> *"The candy. From last time."*
> *(She looks at the player with a clarity she didn't have before.)*
> *"His name was Ramon. He always carried menthol candies. He said they helped him think. We were supposed to meet that night at the corner, and I was running late, andâ€”"*
> *(She stops. Straightens.)*
> *"He's been gone from that corner for a very long time. He is either very old, or he has moved on. Either way, I am the only one still waiting."*
> *(A pause.)*
> *"I don't think it helps anyone if I keep doing that."*
> *(She smiles. It's the first real, full, genuine smile she has given. It's small. But it's completely hers.)*
> *"Your lights are very warm. I want you to know that. It helped. More than you know."*

**Information Out:** Saying his name â€” Ramon â€” is the emotional anchor. The choice to stop waiting is given weight because it was a *choice*, not a resolution handed to her.

---

### Chapter 9 — *"The Departure"*

**Scene Beat:** The epilogue interaction. She isn't a customer today. She is fully ethereal, leaving her final mark.

**Information to Communicate:**

- She has crossed over successfully.
- She leaves the player a permanent token.

**Character Filter — What She Wants:**
To say a final goodbye.

**What Else Is Happening:**
Audio grows very quiet. The lighting on her sprite is brighter, less sharp.

**Key Beat:**
> *"I'm leaving now. I just wanted to say goodbye."*
> *(She reaches out, and though her fingers are translucent, she places a small, dried flower on the counter).*
> *"You have a good heart. Please, keep the lights on for as long as you can. It helps the lost ones find their way."*
> *(She steps back into the dark and slowly fades away completely).*
