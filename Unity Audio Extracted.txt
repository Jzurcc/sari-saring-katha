The Importance of Audio and Sound 
Effects
• Sound design in a game helps with setting the theme and 
atmosphere of various scenes.
• Soundtracks are the music and ambient sounds that you 
hear in a game.
• Sound effects are all non-musical audio that is triggered 
through gameplay elements.
The Importance of Audio and Sound 
Effects
Soundtracks
• Theme songs
• Background music
• Menu music
• Combat music
Sound Effects
• Running sounds
• Interaction sounds
• Weapon swings
• Gear sounds
• Vehicle Sounds
• Voiceovers
• UI/UX sounds
The Importance of Audio and Sound 
Effects
• Soundtracks and sound effects are either typically a mix of 
in-house developments and third-party sources.
• The type of game you create will determine what type of 
audio and sound effects you will use.
• Some games prefer a focus on soundtracks while others 
place heavy emphasis on sound effects.
The Importance of Audio and Sound 
Effects
• There are five key elements in designing the sound for a 
game.
• Source
• Envelope
• Pitch
• Frequency
• Layering
Elements in Sound Design
Source
• This refers to the source or inspiration of your sound effects.
• These can be taught of as the foundation of the various 
audio elements in your game.
• Typically, you want to record real life sounds as a basis for 
sound effects and then edit them later on.

Elements in Sound Design
Envelopes
• This describes the volume of sounds effects over time.
• The two parts of an envelope are attack and release.
• These parts are typically described by their speed.
Elements in Sound Design
Envelopes
• Attack refers to how fast 
the sound volume peaks.
• Release refers to how the 
loudness of the sound and 
how the sound ends.

Elements in Sound Design
Pitch
• This refers to the highness or lowness of the pitch.
• Changing the pitch of a sound will give a “wavering” 
effect if taken to the extreme.
• This is typically tied to the frequency of the sound.
Elements in Sound Design
Pitch
• Large characters for 
example will typically have 
low-pitched voices, while 
small characters will have 
high pitched voices.
• Pitch will also change in 
relation to speed, such as a 
vehicle speeding up or 
slowing down.

Elements in Sound Design
Frequency
• This refers to the rate of vibration for your sound effects.
• Higher frequencies are typically referred to as the treble.
• Lower frequencies are referred to as the bass.
Elements in Sound Design
Frequency
• Using an equalizer is a way 
to visualize frequencies 
and adjust them as 
needed for your game.
• Adjusting frequencies will 
allow sound to either stand 
out or blend into the 
background.

Elements in Sound Design
Layering
• This refers to the process of playing multiple sounds 
together in a unique sequence.
• This is like the concept of mixing, which is combining 
multiple tracks to create new music.
• Compared to mixing, layering is simply stacking different 
sound on top of each other.
Elements in Sound Design
Layering
• This allows you to combine 
two basic sound effects 
and create entire new 
sounds, such as a sword 
and fire, to create an 
enchanted fire sword.

Unity Audio Mixing
• Unity is compatible with the common types of the audio 
files:
• Audio Interchange File Format (AIFF)
• Waveform Audio File Format (WAV)
• MPEG Audio Layer-3 (MP3)
• Ogg Vorbis (OGG)
• Unity also supports tracker modules, which is creating 
instruments from audio samples.
Unity Audio Mixing
• Unity makes use of two parts when implementing audio in 
a game.
• The first is the Audio Source, which dictates where sounds 
will be emitted from.
• The second is the Audio Listener, which picks up sound 
effects from different sources and triggers a response.
Unity Audio Mixing
• You can also make use of Audio Filters to add effects to 
the various sounds in your game.
• These are added as additional components to a 
GameObject.
• These are then applied to the attached audio file when 
the game object is triggered or enabled for a scene.
Adding Audio in Unity
• Adding audio in Unity is very simple.
• First, we will be opening or creating a project to use.
• The project should already have a GameObject that we 
can use as our Audio Source.
Adding Audio in Unity
• Start by creating an 
“Audio” Folder in your 
project assets.
• This is where you will be 
adding your game audio.

Adding Audio in Unity
• To add our audio file/s to 
our project, we can click 
and drag the files to our 
audio folder.
• Wait for a minute or two, 
and the audio will appear 
inside the assets folder in 
Unity.

Adding Audio in Unity
• You can open the audio 
file in the Unity editor and 
inspect its properties.
• The settings shown on the 
right will allow you to adjust 
how the audio is loaded 
and played to optimize its 
performance, but we will 
leave these settings at 
default for now.

Adding Audio in Unity
• Next, we will need to 
create an Audio Source 
which will emit the audio 
file which we just imported 
into our project.
• To do this, we select a 
GameObject and then 
add a new Audio Source 
component to it.

Adding Audio in Unity
• In our Audio source 
component, we can then 
add the audio file by 
selecting Audio Resource 
and then selecting our 
imported file.

Adding Audio in Unity
• Next, we would typically 
add an Audio Listener to 
be able to hear the sound 
but by default, our main 
camera already comes 
with an Audio Listener set 
up.
• With this, we can hear the 
audio from the perspective 
of the camera.

Adding Audio in Unity
• If you play the audio with 
the default component 
settings, you might notice 
that it plays evenly 
throughout your entire 
scene at max volume.
• We can adjust this by 
making changes in the 
inspector.

Adding Audio in Unity
• Of note is the volume, 
which changes the overall 
volume of the sound, and 
Spatial Blend.
• Spatial Blend allows us to 
create a 3D effect for our 
audio, depending on how 
far we adjust the slider.
• The basis for the spatial

Adding Audio in Unity
• With this, we can now 
implement basic sound 
effects and soundtracks in 
our game!
• Take note that Audio

Audio Mixing
Unity Audio
Unity Audio Mixer
• The Audio Mixer allows you to mix audio source, apply 
various effects, and perform audio mastering.
• This is added to our game as a separate asset via the 
assets folder.
• We can then add it to a GameObject via the Audio 
Source component.
Unity Audio Mixer
• To add an Audio Mixer, we 
select Create > Audio > 
Audio Mixer.
• This will create a new Audio 
Mixer asset in our project.
• We can then open the 
asset by double clicking on 
it to see its settings.

Unity Audio Mixer
• Here, we can adjust the 
different options for the 
Audio Mixer, such as 
adding different effects, or 
adding different channels.
• In Unity, we call audio 
channels “groups”.

Unity Audio Mixer
• By itself, the Audio Mixer 
asset does not do 
anything.
• It needs to be attached to 
a GameObject for it to 
function.
• Specifically, we need to 
add it as part of an Audio 
Source component.

Unity Audio Mixer
• We can adjust the audio in 
real time through the Audio 
Mixer while a scene is 
playing.
• To do this, you simply need 
to click Edit in Play Mode 
while a scene is running.
• Any changes you make to 
the Audio Mixer while the 
scene is running are saved.

Unity Audio Mixer
• You can add multiple 
effects to your different 
channels to achieve 
different effects for your 
audio files.
• These effects will apply in 
real time to the audio 
sources in your scene.

Audio Playlists
Unity Audio
Unity Audio Playlists
• The Audio Random Container allows you to set up and 
play a randomized list of audio clips in your game.
• You can also adjust the volume and adjust effects for the 
playlist for all audio clips in it.
• This is typically used for repeating sounds such as 
footsteps, weapons, props, and environmental effects.
Unity Audio Playlists
• To create an Audio 
Random Container, we 
click Create > Audio > 
Audio Random Container 
to add it as an asset to 
your game.
• We then double click the 
newly created asset to add 
audio files to it.

Unity Audio Playlists
• When you open the Audio 
Random Container asset, 
you can click Edit Audio 
Random Container to add 
audio files and edit how 
they are played in your 
game.

Unity Audio Playlists
• When we open the Audio 
Random Container asset, 
we can select the audio 
clips that we would like to 
play randomly in our 
game.
• We can also adjust other 
options for how the volume 
and pitch will vary for our 
audio files.

Unity Audio Playlists
• Once we have added our 
audio clips, we can now 
add it as part of our game.
• To add it to our game, we 
attach it to an Audio 
Source in one of our Game 
Objects.

Unity Audio Playlists
• Like individual audio files, 
we can also adjust the 
attributes of the random 
audio files via the Audio 
Source component or 
modify how it sounds ever 
further with a mixer.
